The issue & our approach

1 The problem statement

Factory workers are exposed to harmful working postures and harmful noise because workstations, tasks and work environments are not designed around human body dimensions, physical tolerance and hearing. The resulting musculoskeletal disorders and hearing loss are slow, invisible and permanent, and they persist because supervisors have no practical way to measure the risk, understand it, act on it, or check that their fixes worked.

Why the risk goes unmanaged

  1. RULA, REBA and noise surveys need trained assessors and paper worksheets.
  2. Noise needs a sound level meter small factories do not own.
  3. Results stay on paper: the supervisor, who can change the workplace, cannot see who is most at risk, what to fix first, or whether a fix worked.

The two checks

✓
Is it a human issue? Yes. The work's heights, loads, durations and sound levels exceed human limits. The fix is to change the work to fit the human.
✓
Can it be a progressive web app? Yes. A phone has a camera (posture), a microphone (noise) and enough power to detect the pose inside the browser. It installs from a link, works offline and needs no app store.

2 The human factors issue

Work should be designed around the capabilities and limits of the human, not the other way round.

In factories the machines, pallets and presses work exactly as designed. What fails is the fit between the work and the human. The work exceeds three human limits:

Body size varies

Limit Indian adults range from about 143 cm (small woman) to 176 cm (tall man).

Work demand One fixed workstation height; items on the floor or above the shoulder.

Result The worker bends, stoops, reaches or shrugs. The awkward posture is caused by the design.

Body tolerates limited load

Limit Muscles, joints and spinal discs cope with neutral postures, not bent, twisted or raised ones held for long.

Work demand Awkward postures held or repeated for hours, with loads and little recovery.

Result Fatigue, then musculoskeletal disorders of the back, neck, shoulders, wrists and knees.

Hearing is fragile

Limit The ear's sensory cells do not regrow, and the ear must also catch speech and alarms.

Work demand Hours in zones above 85 to 90 dB(A): presses, looms, grinders.

Result Permanent hearing loss; masked warnings; more accidents.

Key insight: many harmful postures are not a bad habit. They come from a workstation that does not fit this worker's body. Fix the workstation, not the worker.

  • At workstations whose heights and reach do not match the worker.

  1. Awkward postures, with load, poor grip and repetition.

5 Pros and cons

Pros

  • Only a phone: no sound level meter, no worksheets, no ergonomist needed.
  • Fixes sized to the worker: workstation heights from their own body, not an average.
  • Posture and noise on one scale, with every worker ranked so the supervisor knows who to help first.
  • Checks that fixes work: before/after re-assessment with the reduction shown.
  • Built on the framework: each worker gets their own 4W1H and User vs Work graph.
  • Private and offline: photos never leave the phone.

Cons

  • One 2D photo cannot measure twisting or side-bending; these are entered by hand.
  • RULA and REBA score one moment of a task, not the whole shift.
  • Phone microphones are approximate (about ±2 to 5 dB after calibration).
  • Body dimensions are estimated from height, not measured.
  • Data stays on one device and must be exported.
  • The worker's own comfort and the effect of noise on communication are not measured yet.
